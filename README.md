# Employee Attrition Prediction — PeopleFirst Global Services
### IIT Madras Advanced Certificate in ML, GenAI & LLMs — HR Analytics Capstone

> **Can we predict which employees will leave — before they hand in their notice?**

---

## Project Overview

PeopleFirst Global Services faces **61.4% annual attrition** — significantly above the industry average of 15–20%. HR interventions are currently reactive: by the time an exit interview happens, the decision has already been made.

This project builds a **machine learning system that scores every employee's attrition probability monthly**, enabling proactive, targeted retention interventions before employees disengage.

---

## Key Results

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Best Model** | LightGBM Classifier |
| **ROC-AUC** | 0.9248 (Excellent) |
| **Dataset** | 50,500 employees × 36 features |
| **Industry Benchmark AUC** | 0.75 – 0.85 |

### Top 5 Attrition Drivers (SHAP-confirmed)

| Rank | Feature | Mean \|SHAP\| | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement Score | 1.0681 | Low → High Risk |
| 2 | Manager Relationship Score | 0.9348 | Low → High Risk |
| 3 | Employee Satisfaction Score | 0.9138 | Low → High Risk |
| 4 | Work-Life Balance Score | 0.8316 | Low → High Risk |
| 5 | Years in Current Role | 0.7726 | High → High Risk |

> **Key Insight:** Non-financial factors (engagement, manager quality, satisfaction) outrank salary as attrition predictors. This shifts the retention strategy from compensation-driven to experience-driven.

## Notebook Descriptions

### NB01 — Data Loading & Profiling
Loads the raw dataset and produces a full profile report: shape, dtypes, missing values, cardinality, and class distribution. Confirms 50,500 rows × 36 columns.

**Output:** Console summary, baseline statistics

---

### NB02 — Data Cleaning & Normalization
Drops ID columns, fixes categorical casing inconsistencies, performs grouped-median imputation for missing values, applies ordinal and label encoding, and Z-score scales 21 continuous numeric features.

**Key decisions:**
- `employment_type` casing fix: `Full-time` → `Full-Time`
- Ordinal encoding: `education_level` (1–5), `business_travel_frequency` (1–3)
- `StandardScaler` applied to 21 columns; fit on full cleaned dataset

**Output:** `data/processed/PeopleFirst_cleaned.csv` — 50,500 × 31

---

### NB03 — Exploratory Data Analysis
13 visualization sections covering target distribution, numeric distributions, outlier analysis, attrition by department and role, tenure patterns, compensation analysis, engagement, workload, and correlation heatmap.

**Key EDA findings:**
- All 4 engagement/satisfaction scores are top correlates with staying
- Burnout pattern: high overtime + high absenteeism → high attrition
- Early-tenure (<2 years) employees are highest-risk
- Frequent travelers have notably higher attrition rates

**Output:** Visual summary printed to console, 6 saved charts

---

### NB04 — Feature Engineering & Class Balancing

**5 new engineered features:**

| Feature | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `engagement_wlb_composite` | engagement × work_life_balance | Captures compound low-engagement effect |
| `tenure_bucket` | Quantile bins (Early/Mid/Senior) | Captures non-linear U-shaped tenure risk |
| `below_dept_median_salary` | 1 if salary < dept median | Relative underpayment is stronger signal than absolute |
| `satisfaction_composite` | Mean of 3 satisfaction scores | Single summary of overall wellbeing |
| `burnout_index` | overtime × (1 + absenteeism) | Amplifies simultaneous high-overtime + high-absence |

**Train/Test split:** Stratified 70/30 (no timestamp column available for time-based split)

**SMOTE:** Applied to training set only. Minority class (Stayed, 38.6%) is over-sampled to achieve 50/50 balance. Test set is never touched.

**Output:**
- `X_train.csv`: 43,440 × 35 (SMOTE balanced)
- `X_test.csv`: 15,150 × 35 (original distribution)

---

### NB05 — Model Development

Three models trained using a shared `evaluate_model()` helper with 5-fold Stratified CV:

| Model | Role | CV AUC | Test AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | Interpretable baseline | — | 0.8531 |
| LightGBM | **Primary model** | — | **0.9248** |
| XGBoost | Comparison | — | 0.9248 |

**LightGBM selected as primary** — SHAP-compatible, faster inference, natively handles categorical features, ties with XGBoost but is more interpretable.

**Output:** `models/logistic_regression.pkl`, `lightgbm_model.pkl`, `xgboost_model.pkl`

---

### NB06 — Model Evaluation & SHAP Analysis

Deep dive into LightGBM performance:
- ROC curves for all 3 models
- Confusion matrix analysis
- Threshold sensitivity analysis (0.1–0.9) — shows Recall/Precision/F1 trade-off
- SHAP TreeExplainer on full test set (15,150 employees)
- SHAP beeswarm and bar plots

**Risk segmentation:**

| Tier | Probability | HR Action |
|---|---|---|
| High Risk | > 0.70 | Immediate intervention |
| Medium Risk | 0.40 – 0.70 | Quarterly check-in |
| Low Risk | ≤ 0.40 | Monitor |

**Output:** `reports/employee_risk_scores.csv` — every employee with their attrition probability and risk tier

## Business Recommendations

Based on SHAP analysis findings:

1. **Launch quarterly engagement pulse surveys** — engagement is the #1 driver; measure and act on it
2. **Train managers on retention conversations** — manager relationship is #2; people leave managers, not companies
3. **Invest in recognition & career pathing programs** — satisfaction outranks salary in this dataset
4. **Set overtime caps; monitor WLB by team** — burnout pattern is measurable and preventable
5. **Implement structured 18-month career development cycles** — career stagnation (years in current role) is #5 driver
